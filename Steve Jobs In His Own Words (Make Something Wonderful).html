01 / SEEING
Start here

See what is not there yet.

From Laurene Powell Jobs’ introduction and Jobs in 2007. The core skill: imagine what reality lacks, then fix it.

Core idea (80–120 words): Jobs’ gift was seeing what was missing, not only what was present. He believed the world is built by people no smarter than you, so it is malleable (= you can reshape it). His rule was to marry the arts with technology and design for how things ought to be, not how they are. Ideas came as intuitions from inner freedom, not as arguments, and he applied rigor to himself first through constant drafting. The Macintosh team, he said, could have been writers or musicians — they made a tool for creativity, streamlined and simple, because computers would soon take hours of each day.

  • Step 1 — Notice what is missing, not only what is broken.
  • Step 2 — Picture how it ought to be in plain words.
  • Step 3 — Marry useful with beautiful in one object.
  • Step 4 — Trust the intuition, then refine by drafting.

Everyday examples

What: You build Heathkit-style kits as a kid → So what: you learn finished things are buildable, not magic.

What: Macintosh looks like a friendly typewriter, not blinking lights → So what: people try it because it feels calm.

What: You rewrite a confusing school form in plain words → So what: that is ought-to-be design.

Analogy: Like a conjurer’s trick seen twice — once you know how it got here, you stop being fooled and start building.

❌ What People Get Wrong: Jobs started from data and proofs — he started from intuition, then refined.

💡 Why This Matters: If the world is made, you can remake one small part of it.

🔗 Inter-Module Link: This seeing skill is what lets you attempt Module 2’s tiny first build.

Are → Ought
Design moves from how things are to how they ought to be.
Q: In one sentence, what does “ought-to-be” design mean? A: Design for how life should feel, not how tools happen to look now.

02 / BUILDING
The garage years

Build tiny, sell one batch, repeat.

From the early-Apple story in the talk: Heathkits, the garage in Los Altos, and the first shop order.

Core idea (80–120 words): Young Jobs learned from Heathkits and neighbour Larry Lang that you can open a finished product and build one yourself. With Wozniak he cut computer assembly from about 50 hours of hand wiring to about one hour with a printed circuit board (= a board where parts plug in). They sold a VW bus and a costly calculator to fund parts, walked barefoot into Paul Terrell’s shop, and took an order for 50 fully assembled machines. They bought parts on 30-day credit, delivered, got cash, and paid suppliers. Profit sat as 50 extra machines on the floor — a classic cash crunch.

  • Step 1 — Learn by assembling a kit with your hands.
  • Step 2 — Cut build time with one smart shortcut.
  • Step 3 — Fund it yourself and pre-sell a small batch.
  • Step 4 — Deliver, collect cash, pay suppliers fast.

Everyday examples

What: You sell 20 lunch pre-orders before cooking → So what: you buy food with customer cash, not loans.

What: You make a template that cuts homework from 2 hours to 20 minutes → So what: that is your circuit board.

Analogy: Like baking one tray to pay for the next tray — profit you cannot spend yet is just trays on the floor.

❌ What People Get Wrong: Apple started with investors — it started with a bus sale and supplier credit.

💡 Why This Matters: A small paid batch beats a big unpaid idea.

🔗 Inter-Module Link: When this loop breaks, you need Module 3’s hunger and honesty.

03 / TRUTH
The hard middle

Choose excellence over being right.

From the NeXT and Pixar failures, the 1995 Intel emails with Andy Grove, and the return to Apple.

Core idea (80–120 words): After leaving Apple, Jobs ran two failing firms at once: NeXT computers and Pixar. He told his team to make new and creative mistakes. When an Intel engineer asked Pixar to share graphics secrets for free, Jobs demanded payment — then mentor Andy Grove corrected him: friends share knowledge and it balances out later. Jobs replied he had changed his view and would help. The same honesty brought him back to Apple: he told a director the hard truth about its CEO, and years later that truth earned him an invitation to return with NeXT’s system.

  • Step 1 — Admit both bets are failing at once.
  • Step 2 — Prefer excellence to winning the point.
  • Step 3 — Accept correction from people who built before you.
  • Step 4 — Tell the truth even when it risks your seat.

Everyday examples

What: A teammate proves your plan wrong in review → So what: you switch plans same day to stay excellent.

What: You tell a coach the training is not working → So what: short pain now avoids long failure later.

What: A hire ignores you and pitches Apple to buy NeXT → So what: right people earn room to disobey once.

Analogy: Like a good cook tasting a rival’s soup and copying the salt level — pride never seasoned anything.

❌ What People Get Wrong: Strength means never changing your mind — Jobs changed his the same week.

💡 Why This Matters: Truth keeps failing projects able to turn.

🔗 Inter-Module Link: Honesty about failure protects the short time in Module 4.

Two Failures → Two Foundations
NeXT and Pixar both struggled before they carried the future.
Q: What did Grove teach Jobs about sharing secrets? A: Share freely among builders; help given now returns later without a bill.

04 / TIME
How to spend it

Blaze briefly, then make it count.

From the rainbow speech, the 2003 business-school talk, the 2005 Stanford address, and the resignation letter.

Core idea (80–120 words): “You appear, blaze in the sky, then disappear,” Jobs told students. Picture life as a rainbow: birth and death are its two fixed ends, both faced alone. Use that end as a tool — ask what you would do if time were short, and say no to the rest. Apple, he said, might invent twelve things a year but advertise only four, because buyers can hold one new idea at a time. Do only work you love, keep looking until you find it, ask busy elders for quarterly lunches, and study what came before so the human story under history shows.

  • Step 1 — Remember the rainbow ends: time is fixed.
  • Step 2 — Keep 4 messages from 12 ideas; drop the rest.
  • Step 3 — Love the work or keep looking; do not settle.
  • Step 4 — Ask an elder to lunch; learn the past.

Everyday examples

What: You list 12 club goals, keep 4 posters → So what: members remember and join one thing.

What: You email a retired builder for a 30-minute lunch → So what: most say yes, like Grove and Packard did.

What: You quit a project you dread to keep one you love → So what: grit lasts only where love fuels it.

Analogy: Like packing one small bag for a short trip — the limit forces you to keep only what matters.

❌ What People Get Wrong: “Stay hungry” means work longer — it means stay curious and keep standards high.

💡 Why This Matters: Short time plus focus is how wonderful things ship.

🔗 Inter-Module Link: Focused time lets you re-attempt Module 1’s ought-to-be vision without waste.

FAQ

Did Jobs really work barefoot and sell his bus? ▾

Yes, in this telling: he sold his VW bus, Wozniak sold his calculator, and Jobs walked barefoot into Terrell’s shop to land 50 assembled machines.

Why did HP and Atari say no to Apple? ▾

Neither saw a personal-computer firm inside their firm. Bushnell later called passing on Apple stock his biggest regret.

What did Grove actually correct? ▾

Charging Pixar’s helpers. Grove said builders share freely and it balances out; Jobs agreed within days.

How do “love what you do” and focus fit? ▾

Love fuels 12 hard years; focus picks which 4 of 12 ideas deserve that love each year. Same logic shaped Apple Stores: invite the 95% who never bought, not only the 5% who had.

What were his lasting values, and who took over? ▾

Life is short, tell the truth, tools must lift creativity, and process, beauty, and details all matter. His 2011 letter named Tim Cook as successor and said Apple’s brightest days were ahead.
