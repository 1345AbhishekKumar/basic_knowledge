      SYS.01
      
CEO Core
The kernel: ikigai, vision & the personal board meeting

      HOOK · L·N·P·G
    

    

      

        
Every operating system boots from a kernel, and this one is the founder's own psychology. The first tool is ikigai — the Japanese concept of "your reason for being," living at the intersection of four circles: what you love, what the world needs, what you can be paid for, and what you're great at. The CEO's own list includes building teams, design and systems, traveling the world, coaching founders, building community, and building distribution from day one — because first-time founders focus on tech, while second-time founders focus on distribution. Once ikigai is written down, every core action is measured against it, and anything outside the circles — even profitable things — gets automated, eliminated, or delegated.

        
The second pillar is relentless learning: mentors, masterminds, books, coaches and courses account for roughly 95% of the results. Highlights from a Kindle flow through Readwise into Notion — a searchable second brain that doubles as a content-idea engine.

        
"Without a vision you're going to go nowhere. But a vision only works when it's translated to your day-to-day."

        
The third pillar makes vision operational. Public companies have boards holding the CEO accountable; private founders don't — so the personal board meeting was invented to close that gap. Every morning and every night: mission, vision, ikigai, personal 30-day goals and business 30-day goals, reviewed from a printed page. Since ~95% of decisions are subconscious, this daily review programs the subconscious to execute at a blistering pace. A monthly reflection (what went well / didn't / what I learned) surfaces gaps, and a 5-year reflection — advice to a younger self — doubles as an endless content well. Ikigai plus 5-year reflection makes the content hamster wheel disappear.

      

      

        
ANALOGY
Ikigai is the kernel; the PBM is the nightly reboot. Without them, every app (tactic, tool, hustle) eventually crashes — because nothing underneath is holding the machine together.

The intersection is the kernel of the OS. Every core action optimizes for it. Anything outside — even profitable things — gets automated, eliminated, or delegated.


    

    

      
Interactive · The Personal Board Meeting agenda

      

        
        
        
        
      

      
Mission & vision, printed in the notebook. Reviewed every morning and every night. This isn't motivation — it's repetition with intent, honing the subconscious toward the vision so momentum compounds at a blistering pace.

      
Two goal sets, always visible: personal 30-day goals and business 30-day goals — reverse-engineered from 10-year → 3-year → 1-year → 90-day plans, then "getting after it."

      SYS.02
      
Finance
The CEO scorecard: six numbers in one weekly text

      HOOK · R·C·M·C·S²
    

    

      

        
Creative founders often neglect finance — it isn't their favorite thing — and that neglect quietly kills companies. The fix isn't becoming a finance person. It's a single system called the CEO scorecard. Every week, the CFO sends one text message with the six numbers that define business health: revenue, cost of goods sold, profit margin, cash in bank, show rate, close rate. One glance and the CEO knows whether the machine is healthy — no dashboards to log into, no 40-page report to pretend to read.

        
Then comes the part that makes it transformative: the CFO adds 3–5 bullets — "if I were in your shoes, here's what I'd focus on next week." When the text lands Friday afternoon, the CEO implements every single bullet before signing off for the weekend. Saturday arrives with the business already corrected. In his words: absolute game-changer.

        
"It makes it effortless for me to understand the health of the businesses that I'm operating."

        
The structure underneath matters too. Finance manager Georgie handles invoices, payments and general accounting — the simple stuff — so CFO Hayden's attention is reserved for work only a CFO can do. The scorecard is a forcing function that puts your CFO to work, converts finance from a chore into a weekly ritual, and gives a creative CEO effortless clarity. If a founder installs exactly one system from this entire lesson, this is the one he guarantees will transform the business.

      

      

        
ANALOGY
The scorecard is a dashboard warning light. You don't need to be a mechanic to drive the car — you need the light to come on when oil pressure drops, and a mechanic who tells you what to do about it.

      SYS.03
      
Sales
The offer stack & the slippery slope

      HOOK · B→N→C→C
    

    

      

        
Most founders believe they sell one offer: the product. That is the misconception. In reality you have an offer stack — three to seven offers arranged along the journey from stranger to customer. Your bio is an offer (follow me). Your newsletter landing page is an offer (take my email). The CTA inside the newsletter is an offer (book a call). The pitch on the call is an offer (join). When every rung is labeled and its copy dialed word-by-word, sentence-by-sentence, the stack becomes a slippery slope: two weeks after watching one YouTube video, people "mistakenly" end up in your community — the journey is simply that smooth.

        
The numbers prove the point. An average bio converts ~5% of profile visitors into followers; Founder OS converts ~20%. An average newsletter page converts ~0.5%; theirs converts ~5% — a 10× lift from copy alone.

        
"People just end up almost mistakenly in your funnel, because that journey is just so damn smooth."

        
The squad has two layers. Setters (SDRs) usually hunt leads — but inbound is so heavy they screen instead, running a "Harvard for entrepreneurship" bar that rejects anyone who isn't a perfect fit. Brand strategists then guide qualified founders across the line. The CEO stays in the mix — Monday and Friday meetings, end-of-day reports, projections — because sales is the company's best intel engine: five reps × 200 conversations a month is a thousand data points on objections, desires, and where the product should evolve. Every script, payment plan and follow-up sequence is a marketing asset — because at that volume, the sales team is the marketing engine.

      

      

        
ANALOGY
The offer stack is a waterslide. You don't drag people up the stairs of your product — you build a slope so smooth that, once they step in at the bio, gravity does the selling.

        

          

            
⚠ MISCONCEPTION
"I have one offer — my product."
So the bio, the newsletter page and the call script all get written as afterthoughts.

✓ REALITY
You have 3–7 offers.
Bio → newsletter → call → close. Label each one, dial the copy on each one, and conversion compounds at every rung — 4× to 10× industry average.

If the scorecard is the most transformative finance system, founder success is the most important department of all. Its flagship install is the Content GPS: a systemized way to scale on X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram and YouTube — an SOP for every platform — driving audiences through core CTAs into the newsletter, into workshops, into revenue. When a founder joins, the GPS is live in their business within four weeks, and it works for course creators, SaaS companies, coaches, consultants and agencies alike.

        
Around it orbits a constellation of systems. Founder OS90 builds a 90-day infrastructure of posts, CTAs and DM sequences for predictable growth. Launch OS compresses 15 years of seven- and eight-figure launches into eight gears — landing page, social, newsletter, funnel, metrics, assets, workshop, emails — so that "your launch can't fail." The Content Idea Machine produces 30 ideas in 30 minutes from eight prompts: tools, facts, systems, guides, playbooks, quotes, stories, reasons. In total, 180+ systems hum behind this department.

        
"People don't buy what you sell — they buy what others want to buy. Proof is your most underrated asset."

        
So wins are captured systemically — on Trustpilot and testimonial.to — and converted into marketing collateral, almost effortlessly. Four founder success managers run twice-weekly meetings and call reviews, coaching gaps in real time; and any system a client needs that doesn't yet exist bubbles up and gets built. Finally, ikigai coaching moves founders from scarcity to abundance — because a founder building a business they hate is the saddest thing in this industry.

      SYS.05
      
Studios
You are a media company — act like its CEO

      HOOK · 1 → 24
    

    

      

        
Here's the reframe that powers the whole department: you are not a founder who occasionally makes content. You are a media company — and you'd better be its CEO. The mission is "Pixar for founders": the most inspiring founder content in the world, built by a creative director who is one part taste and one part detail. Taste — a strong brand identity — is the ultimate competitive advantage. Detail makes the CEO the chief detail officer, reviewing creative metrics every single week.

        
The squad translates one brand through every medium: editors for short-form and long-form, a motion designer who owns the animation language, graphic designers, and a YouTube producer who runs ideation, packaging, filming and string-outs. Done right, the output feels like it came from one person — same green, same animation style, same color grade, same B-roll. That consistency is what gets programmed into an audience of 80M+ monthly views.

        
"90% of the success of any YouTube video is the packaging — not the video."

        
So every video gets ~15 thumbnails, an AI pass, and six months of A/B testing via Thumbnail Test and TubeBuddy; titles are engineered for curiosity and intrigue; and outliers are scouted on oneof.ten.com as packaging R&D. Ideas are de-risked on X first — one thread, and if it has steam, the content waterfall turns it into 24 assets. The founder handles only ideation, scripting and filming; everything after is delegated — editing is always the first thing to go (find a badass editor at ytjob.co). The content calendar sets the quantity ceiling — X twice daily, LinkedIn daily, Shorts daily, a reel and a carousel daily, TikTok daily, long-form every other week — and after that, only quality matters.

      

      

        
ANALOGY
You're not a founder with a camera — you're a media company with a product. The content isn't marketing for the business. The business is distribution for the content.

        

          

            
⚠ MISCONCEPTION
"Post more. Volume wins."
So founders burn out chasing a quantity that was never the bottleneck.
CLICK TO FLIP →

            
✓ REALITY
The calendar is a ceiling, not a floor.
Post at the ceiling, then raise the bar on quality — because 90% of any video's success is decided by the packaging before a single second is watched.

      SYS.06
      
Marketing & Technology
Rented audience → owned audience, on $0 ads

      HOOK · 16 NETS · 6.3×
    

    

      

        
Marketing runs under Nicholas, with Niels on the newsletter and Neva editing the upcoming book. The newsletter is not a run-of-the-mill twice-a-week blast — it's a logic engine: an entire email infrastructure with sequences branching on what each subscriber clicks. The founder calls it the most powerful part of the entire marketing machine, and insists that founders without deep email logic are simply leaving money on the table.

        
The engine is fed by the CTA system — a net with roughly 16 locations across platforms: Instagram bio and stories, LinkedIn featured section and cover photos, the endings of X and LinkedIn posts, YouTube descriptions and more. That net, spending zero dollars on ads, drives ~20,000 newsletter subscribers a month and grew the list to 172,000 in two years.

        
"You don't own LinkedIn. You don't own X, Instagram or YouTube. Drive the rented audience into the audience you own."

        
The math is brutal: a post reaches maybe 10% of a rented audience, if you're lucky. A newsletter send lands in ~63% of an owned one — every single person, in the inbox. Below it all sits technology, run by Dawn — a Florida-man CTO based in the Philippines — with a Webflow engineer and two VAs. His mandate: make HubSpot run like an absolute machine, tracking every human from awareness to transaction to renewal to referral to testimonial, so the entire business lives in one system. Even retreats are systemized — twice a year, scaling to four, then twelve, with a retreat center in the Dolomites. And the whole OS — all 38 systems — ships as a free lead magnet. That's the founder freedom movement.

      

      

        
ANALOGY
Social platforms are rented apartments — the landlord can change the locks, the algorithm, the rent. The newsletter is the house you own, with your name on the deed and 63% of the household home at dinner.
