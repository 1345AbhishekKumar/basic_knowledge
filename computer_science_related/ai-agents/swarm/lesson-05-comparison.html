Lesson 05 · Comparison · Teams vs Swarm vs Deep Research

Three organizations, one trade: breadth vs control

Sean’s synthesis: all three reconcile multiple opinions to avoid bias. Teams gives you CEO control over pre-defined specialists. Swarm gives autonomous decomposition for vague prompts. Deep Research adds clarification + brief ceremony. Choice depends on how crisply you can define the job.

Sean’s Verdict — Teams Excels When…

  • Determined workflow — you are the CEO / board
  • Want full control: who researches what lens
  • Token-sensitive but need breadth — can steer scouts down to min tools

Swarm Excels When…

  • Very vague intent — “you want it to break down by itself”
  • Require autonomous decomposition without pre-definition
  • Need maximal breadth; accept 2× token cost for random-forest-style coverage

Common Ground

  • Both reconcile differing conclusions centrally — not single-agent bias
  • Both enable batch file processing / talking to many agents at once
  • Both move us from “agent” to “agent organization / society”
Explanation — the quadrant is a decision tool, not a ranking

Teams vs Swarm vs Deep Research: breadth on Y, control on X

Sean’s synthesis at 15:40: “Agent swarm would excel at tasks that require parallel processing without predefinition… If your task requires a determined workflow and you want the system to run exactly the way you want, like you're the board member, then Teams would do a good task… both have batch processing… allow a bunch of agents to have different opinions… need to reconcile.” The diagram puts that on two axes so you stop arguing which is “better” and start asking what you can define.

How the three circles were placed

Swarm top-left (high breadth, low control): 12 spawned, none pre-defined — breadth is maximal, control is “vague intent → let it decide.” Teams bottom-right (high control, medium breadth): 5 pre-defined scouts, leader-enforced — control is maximal, breadth capped at what you named before execution. Deep Research center (medium/medium): supervisor + brief ceremony — you get a brief to audit before breadth, but you pay the ceremony time. Single/Sequential bottom-left is kept small because they are not part of this trade — they are the baseline you leave when you need either axis.

Reading the table below the quadrant

The table answers the same question four ways: Who defines workers? How much parallelism was observed? What ceremony is paid? Where is dissent reconciled? The last row is the only prescriptive one — “Best when…” — and it quotes Sean verbatim. Do not memorize the circles; memorize the row “Who defines workers?” If you cannot answer that sentence for Teams, you should not use Teams.

Heuristic — Sean’s one-liner

“Too vague? Let it swarm. Need control? Lead the team.” If your prompt contains “break down by itself,” that is a swarm prompt. If it contains “Teammate 1: … Teammate 2: …”, that is a teams prompt. Anything with “clarify then brief” is Deep Research.

Shared invariant — why organizations, not agents

All three reconcile multiple opinions into one deliverable. That is the move from “agent” to “AI society” Sean names at 16:20: “coding agent is strong… general agent is strong… now we're at the position where we create agents that specialize too… and then the final layer merges.” The batch powers (search + download + files in parallel) are identical; the difference is who gets to be wrong before the merge.

Common misread to avoid

The quadrant does not say Swarm is always broader in quality. It says Swarm is broader in autonomous decomposition. If your decomposition is correct, Teams can be broader in quality because its breadth is directed. The test is: do you have the decomposition before you start?
