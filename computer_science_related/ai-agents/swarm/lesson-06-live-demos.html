Explanation — what the side-by-side actually proved

Demo 1: same YouTube prompt, two cost profiles; Demo 2: same product, five judgments

Demo 1 prompt (verbatim from transcript): “Explore those channels that do developer tools, automation creations, indie hacking… return four channels with names and URLs.” Demo 2 prompt: “Give me feedback on Automatis — WhatsApp-first AI sales agent for consultative made-to-order sales — from VC, product, GTM, customer success, legal.”

Demo 1 — why the token trace is the lesson

Kimi lane (top): Goal → 12 templated researchers → Jane 4 edu channels (Robert Miles, Jeremy Howard, W&B, AI Uncovered) → 10/12 OK → Bowen clusters 26 channels. Findings bar: dev/coder oversaturated, non-tech founders underserved, business automation underserved — three white-space opportunities that both systems later agree on. Claw lane (bottom): 5 scouts dispatched with 70–80 tools → 90k tokens. Human steer: “Don't overthink. 4 channels max, min tool usage.” → scouts 1–2 returned, 3 re-launched with 3 tools each → 15k tokens, same 18m14s wall-clock. The diagram draws that steer as a dashed coral back-edge because it is human control, not autonomous spawn.

Demo 1 — what both surfaced and why it matters that they agreed

  • Spanish market gap: explainer/news exists, zero hands-on “ship from zero” builder channel — actionable for Automanis LatAm customers (ES/PT).
  • Happy-path bias: “Every channel shows happy path. Nobody documents what broke.”
  • Tool advocacy bias: “Every creator picks one tool and advocates. Impartial comparison seat is empty.” — validates Sean’s own video doing the comparison today.
  • Money + rigorous comparison white-space: both reports recommend it, independent of architecture.

Demo 2 — the five lenses as a Teams replay

The Automatis star in the center is coral because it is the only evaluated artifact. The five surrounding boxes repeat the Teams pattern: VC (defensibility is thin), Product (WhatsApp = 2B users, channel-smart), GTM (Latin America ES/PT), Customer Success (missed leads → captured), Legal (complex sales risk). The three takeaway boxes at the bottom are the synthesis the leader would write: flat $200–300 SaaS → per-qualified-lead at month 3 after “qualified” is defined from data; channel-agnostic architecture, don’t overbuild SMS week one (US-only); moat is lead structuring, not chat wrapping.

What to copy for YouTube research

  • Run the same 4-channel prompt on both — keep the 3-tool cap from the start to avoid the 90k burn.
  • Require the goal agent to emit “specs: top 20, by positioning & format” before spawn — that is where Kimi’s clusters come from.
  • Score outputs on bias: does the clustering surface the Spanish gap and the impartial-comparison gap? If not, the decomposition was wrong.

What to copy for Automatis-style review

  • Pre-define the five lenses as Teams teammates — do not let the swarm invent them.
  • Force pricing advice to be staged: flat now, % of conversion after qualified-lead definition — mirrors the investor advice in the transcript.
  • Keep the “not just another chatbot” bar: moat is structured lead capture, not model quality.

Limitations Sean names explicitly

“It took 20 minutes in total. 18 minutes 14 seconds.” The streaming UI (Kimi) vs terminal UI (Claw) polish gap matters for audit. And “it could also consume a lot of tokens too” — the batch powers that save time cost tokens. The trade is not free breadth; it is breadth you pay for and must reconcile.
