Peripherals, Cables, Display Tech & Mobile Systems

Master modern peripheral connection standards (USB-C, Thunderbolt, HDMI, Ethernet), projector deployment, mobile System on a Chip (SoC) architecture, battery health management, and display technologies (IPS, TN, VA, OLED, AMOLED, Micro-LED).

🔌 1. USB Generations & Connector Types

The Universal Serial Bus (USB) standard connects external peripherals to computing systems:

Standard / Connector Max Bandwidth Primary Characteristics & Use Cases
USB 2.0 (High-Speed) 480 Mbps Standard black/white USB Type-A port. Keyboards, mice, flash drives, basic printers.
USB 3.0 / 3.1 Gen 1 (SuperSpeed) 5 Gbps Distinguished by a blue plastic insert inside the Type-A port. External backup drives, webcams.
USB 3.1 Gen 2 (SuperSpeed+) 10 Gbps Often red/teal inserts. Fast external SSDs, high-resolution video capture.
USB-C Up to 20 Gbps Symmetrical, reversible connector. Carries high data bandwidth and up to 100W–240W Power Delivery (USB-PD).
USB4 / Thunderbolt 3 & 4 40 Gbps Uses USB-C physical port. Transmits PCIe data, dual 4K/8K display streams, and high-wattage device charging.
Micro USB & Lightning 480 Mbps Micro-USB for legacy non-Apple mobile devices; Lightning for proprietary Apple iPhones/iPads.

🌐 2. Communication, Power & Legacy Connectors

🖧 RJ-45 & RJ-11 Connectors

• RJ-45 (8-pin): Connects twisted-pair Ethernet cables (Cat5e/Cat6) for LAN networks and DSL modems.

• RJ-11 (4/6-pin): Connects telephone wires for POTS (Plain Old Telephone Service) and analog dial-up.

⚡ Molex & DB89 Connectors

• Molex (4-pin): Legacy internal power connector providing +12V and +5V to IDE hard drives and optical disc drives.

• DB89 / Serial: Legacy D-sub connectors for early mice, joysticks, and serial terminal consoles.

📺 F-Type & Fiber-Optic Cables

• F-Type Coaxial: Screw-on connector used for cable modems and broadband cable television.

• Fiber-Optic (LC / SC / ST): Uses pulses of light across glass fibers for ultra-high-bandwidth, long-distance communication.

🛠️ Punch Down Blocks (110 / 66 Block)

Terminal strips in telecommunications closets where technicians terminate copper pairs to create organized patch panel connections.

📽️ 3. Display Interfaces & Projector Deployment

Display Port Signals Carried Key Notes
HDMI Digital Video + Multi-Channel Audio Most universal consumer display standard (Standard, Mini HDMI, Micro HDMI).
DisplayPort (DP) Digital Video + High-Def Audio Common on PC graphics cards and business monitors; supports daisy-chaining multiple monitors.
DVI (Digital Visual Interface) Digital Video (DVI-D) / Analog (DVI-A) Transmits video only; requires separate 3.5mm audio jack for sound.
VGA (Video Graphics Array) Analog Video (15-pin D-sub) Legacy standard; susceptible to signal noise and ghosting over long cable runs.
Projector Troubleshooting in IT Support
  • Native Resolution Mismatch: If projector displays blurry or cropped images, manually set the OS output resolution to match the projector's native spec (e.g. 1920×1080).
  • Keystone Calibration: Adjust keystone settings to correct trapezoidal geometric distortion caused by projection angle.
  • Thermal Protection: High-intensity halogen projector lamps generate extreme heat. Clean air filters regularly to prevent automatic thermal emergency shutdowns. Modern projectors utilize cool LED or laser engines.

📱 4. Mobile Devices & System on a Chip (SoC)

Mobile devices (smartphones, tablets, smartwatches) operate under strict thermal, physical volume, and battery constraints. Instead of using separate motherboards, discrete graphics cards, and DIMM slots, they use System on a Chip (SoC) architecture:

Definition: System on a Chip (SoC)

A System on a Chip (SoC) integrates the CPU, Graphics Processing Unit (GPU), system RAM, storage controller, wireless modem (5G/Wi-Fi/Bluetooth), and Neural Processing Unit (NPU) all onto a single silicon die. This drastically reduces power consumption, eliminates bus latency, and minimizes physical footprint.

🔋 5. Rechargeable Batteries & Charge Cycles

Most mobile devices and laptops rely on Lithium-Ion (Li-ion) or Lithium-Polymer (LiPo) rechargeable batteries:

  • Charge Cycle: Defined as one complete discharge of 100% battery capacity followed by a full recharge (e.g. discharging from 100% to 50% on Monday, recharging to 100%, and discharging to 50% on Tuesday equals 1 full charge cycle).
  • Chemical Aging: As cycle counts increase (typically after 300–500 cycles), internal resistance rises, causing reduced run time and slower peak charging rates.
  • Safe Operating Temperature: Operating devices in extreme heat (>35°C / 95°F) accelerates degradation and risks battery swelling (gas buildup). Swollen batteries present extreme fire/rupture risks and must be isolated and recycled immediately.

📺 6. Comprehensive Display Technologies

Technology Emission Type Strengths & Positives Weaknesses & Flaws
IPS LCD (In-Plane Switching) Passive (LED Backlit) Superb color reproduction, wide 178° viewing angles, excellent contrast. Preferred for graphic design and photography. Higher manufacturing cost, slower response times than TN, potential "IPS glow" at sharp viewing angles.
TN LCD (Twisted Nematic) Passive (LED Backlit) Very low cost, ultra-fast response times (1ms), high refresh rates for competitive esports gaming. Narrow viewing angles, color shifting, poor color accuracy and shallow contrast.
VA LCD (Vertical Alignment) Passive (LED Backlit) High contrast ratios (3000:1+), deep black levels for media consumption and movies. Prone to motion blur and pixel ghosting in fast-paced visual transitions.
OLED / AMOLED Active / Emissive (Organic Diodes) Every pixel emits its own light. True infinite contrast (pure 0-nit blacks), ultra-thin, flexible/foldable, instant response time. Susceptible to permanent image retention (burn-in), blue sub-pixel degradation over time, expensive to produce.
Mini-LED (mLED) Passive (Dense Backlit) Uses thousands of 50-60 µm LEDs for granular local dimming zones. Extreme peak brightness (>1600 nits) without OLED burn-in risk. Thicker than OLED, higher cost than standard LCDs.
Micro-LED (μLED) Active / Emissive (Inorganic Diodes) 15 µm microscopic inorganic diodes. Perfect blacks, extreme brightness, no burn-in risk, decades-long lifespan. Extremely complex to manufacture at scale; currently expensive and limited mass production.
Display Refresh Rate (Hz)

The refresh rate refers to how many times per second the display panel draws a completely new image, measured in Hertz (Hz). A 60Hz display updates 60 times/sec, while a 144Hz screen updates 144 times/sec, delivering fluid motion and reducing visual eye fatigue.

📋 7. IT Support Policies: BYOD, RMA & Repair Protocols

BYOD (Bring Your Own Device)

Corporate policy allowing staff to use personal smartphones and laptops for work. IT specialists must enforce Mobile Device Management (MDM), corporate data sandboxing, and strict privacy partitions.

RMA (Return Merchandise Authorization)

The structured vendor warranty tracking process for sending defective hardware back to the manufacturer for certified repair or replacement.

Factory Reset & Data Sanitization

Before releasing or shipping any mobile device for repair, technician must perform a full factory reset to wipe sensitive company credentials, proprietary emails, and user data.
