Lesson 03: Ethernet Standards, Cabling & Duplex

Comprehensive deep dive into IEEE 802.3 physical layer standards, copper UTP categories (Cat5e to Cat7), T568A/B pinout color coding, Straight-Through vs Crossover logic, Auto MDI-X, Single-Mode vs Multi-Mode fiber, and Half-Duplex CSMA/CD collision handling.

📜 1. IEEE 802.3 Ethernet Standards Architecture

Conceived by Bob Metcalfe in 1973 at Xerox PARC and standardized by the IEEE 802.3 working group, Ethernet is the dominant Layer 1 and Layer 2 local area network technology globally:

Data Rate Common Name IEEE Standard Cabling / Physical Medium Max Transmission Distance
10 Mbps Ethernet IEEE 802.3 (10BASE-T) Copper UTP (Cat3/5) 100 meters (328 ft)
100 Mbps Fast Ethernet IEEE 802.3u (100BASE-TX) Copper UTP (Cat5/5e) 100 meters (328 ft)
1000 Mbps (1 Gbps) Gigabit Ethernet IEEE 802.3ab (1000BASE-T) Copper UTP (Cat5e/6) 100 meters (328 ft)
1000 Mbps (1 Gbps) Gigabit Fiber IEEE 802.3z (1000BASE-LX) Optical Fiber (SMF / MMF) 5,000 meters (5 km)
10 Gbps 10 Gigabit Ethernet IEEE 802.3an (10GBASE-T) Copper UTP (Cat6a/7) 100 meters (328 ft)
💡 Standardized Framing Across Mixed Physical Layers

A corporate enterprise network can interconnect hosts running 100BASE-TX, 1000BASE-T, and 10 Gbps inter-switch fiber links simultaneously. Because they all share the standard IEEE 802.3 Ethernet frame format, frames are switched seamlessly without Layer 2 protocol conversion!

⚡ 2. Copper Physics, UTP/STP & Category Specifications

Copper cabling transmits binary data using electrical voltage differentials. Current flows across a continuous circuit consisting of two matched copper conductors. High voltage represents a binary 1, and low voltage represents a binary 0.

Canceling EMI & Crosstalk Through Twisting

External electromagnetic interference (EMI) from motors, florescent lighting, and adjacent wire pairs (crosstalk) is eliminated by twisting copper wire pairs together. Each pair has a unique twist rate. The opposing magnetic fields created by the electrical signals cancel out interference (differential signaling).

UTP vs STP vs Coaxial Construction

• UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair): Most common, cost-effective, flexible.
• STP (Shielded Twisted Pair): Adds metallic foil shielding around individual pairs and a copper drain wire for harsh industrial EMI environments.
• Coaxial (Thinnet 10BASE2 / Thicknet 10BASE5): Legacy central copper core surrounded by dielectric and braided shield.

🔀 4. Straight-Through, Crossover Cables & Auto MDI-X

In 10BASE-T and 100BASE-TX networking, devices fall into two distinct transmission categories:

  • End Hosts & Routers (MDI): Transmit on Pins 1 & 2; Receive on Pins 3 & 6.
  • Switches & Hubs (MDI-X): Transmit on Pins 3 & 6; Receive on Pins 1 & 2.
Cable Type End Pinout Termination Connects Which Devices? Example Device Pairings
Straight-Through Cable T568B to T568B (or T568A to T568A) Unlike Devices (MDI to MDI-X) • PC to Switch
• Router to Switch
• Server to Switch
• PC to Hub
Crossover Cable T568A on one end, T568B on the other Like Devices (MDI to MDI or MDI-X to MDI-X) • PC to PC
• Switch to Switch
• Router to Router
• PC to Router (both are MDI!)
💡 Auto MDI-X (Automatic Medium-Dependent Interface Crossover)

Modern Gigabit and 10Gbps Ethernet NICs and Cisco switch ports support Auto MDI-X, which electronically detects the transmit and receive pairs of the attached cable and automatically configures internal circuitry, allowing straight-through cables to connect any device pairing.

🔦 5. Fiber Optics (SMF vs MMF) & SFP Transceivers

Fiber optic cabling transmits binary signals as pulses of light generated by lasers or LEDs through a high-purity glass core using total internal reflection. Fiber is completely immune to electrical noise (EMI/RFI) and spans kilometers:

Single-Mode Fiber (SMF) — Yellow Jacket

• Core Diameter: Narrow (~9 microns).
• Light Source: Expensive Laser diodes (1310nm / 1550nm).
• Path: Single straight ray of light (zero modal dispersion).
• Distance: Long-haul campus, MAN, and WAN links up to 40+ km.

Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF) — Aqua/Orange Jacket

• Core Diameter: Wide (50 to 62.5 microns).
• Light Source: Low-cost LEDs or VCSELs (850nm / 1300nm).
• Path: Multiple angles of light rays (bounces causing modal dispersion).
• Distance: Datacenter and intra-building links up to 300 to 500 meters.

Cisco switches feature hot-swappable SFP (Small Form-factor Pluggable) and SFP+ (10G) transceiver slots allowing network engineers to insert copper or fiber modules as needed without replacing the switch.

🛑 6. Duplex Modes & CSMA/CD Collision Detection

The transition from legacy Ethernet Hubs to modern Layer 2 Switches revolutionized bandwidth efficiency:

Duplex Mode Operating Characteristics Collision Potential Media Access Protocol
Half Duplex Device can either transmit OR receive at one time, but NOT simultaneously. Shared physical bus (Legacy Hubs). High (Collisions occur if 2 nodes send at once) CSMA/CD (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection)
Full Duplex Device transmits AND receives simultaneously on dedicated point-to-point transmit/receive pairs. (Modern Switches). ZERO (Collisions impossible) None required (CSMA/CD is completely disabled)

The CSMA/CD 5-Step Collision Resolution Process

  1. Carrier Sense (Listen): The host listens to the electrical voltage on the copper bus to confirm the wire is idle.
  2. Multiple Access (Transmit): Any station can access the wire once idle.
  3. Collision Detection (Sense Spike): If two hosts transmit at the same time, their electrical signals collide, creating a voltage spike. Both NICs detect the collision.
  4. Jamming Signal: The detecting stations immediately broadcast an electrical 32-bit Jam Signal across the medium to notify all connected nodes that a collision occurred.
  5. Random Backoff Clock: Each transmitting node initializes a random exponential backoff timer before attempting to listen and retransmit, preventing continuous re-collision!

📝 8. CCNA 200-301 Official Quiz & Knowledge Check

1. Which Ethernet standard supports 1 Gbps data transmission over copper UTP cabling up to 100 meters?
A. 100BASE-TX
B. 1000BASE-T (Correct)
C. 1000BASE-LX
D. 10GBASE-T
Explanation: 1000BASE-T (IEEE 802.3ab) provides 1 Gbps transmission over 4-pair Cat5e/6 copper UTP up to 100 meters. 1000BASE-LX is optical fiber.
2. Which cable type should you use to connect a PC directly to a Cisco Router's Ethernet port without Auto MDI-X?
A. Straight-Through Cable
B. Crossover Cable (Correct)
C. Rollover Cable
D. Coaxial Cable
Explanation: Both PCs and Routers are MDI devices that transmit on Pins 1 & 2 and receive on Pins 3 & 6. Connecting them directly requires a Crossover cable to cross the transmit pairs to the receive pairs.
3. What is the primary cause of signal degradation over long distances in Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF)?
A. Crosstalk
B. Electromagnetic Interference (EMI)
C. Modal Dispersion (Correct)
D. Voltage drop
Explanation: In multi-mode fiber, light rays enter the wide core at multiple angles. Over distance, these rays arrive at slightly different times, causing modal dispersion which blurs the pulses.
4. What is the pinout color sequence for Pin 1 and Pin 2 in the T568A wiring standard?
A. White/Orange, Orange
B. White/Green, Green (Correct)
C. White/Blue, Blue
D. White/Brown, Brown
Explanation: In the T568A standard, Pin 1 is White/Green and Pin 2 is Green. In T568B, Pins 1 and 2 are White/Orange and Orange.
5. Under what condition is CSMA/CD completely disabled on an Ethernet interface?
A. When connecting to a hub
B. When using Cat6 cabling
C. When the interface operates in Full Duplex mode (Correct)
D. When Auto MDI-X is turned off
Explanation: In Full Duplex mode, dedicated transmit and receive paths eliminate collisions entirely; therefore, CSMA/CD collision detection is disabled.
